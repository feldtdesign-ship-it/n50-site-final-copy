Building systems tested in Alaska

Who we are and what we do.

North 50 develops and tests complete building systems for demanding conditions. We use field measurements and engineering analysis to evaluate how their components perform together. Our current work focuses on complete window systems tested through Interior Alaska winters.

Based in Fairbanks, Alaska, North 50 takes its name from the 50th parallel. We study installed building systems under actual conditions to understand how their components work together.

For windows, that means evaluating the glass, frame, cladding, gaskets, seals, and installation as one system. We compare published performance data with real-world measurements from installed assemblies.

Our assurance mission

To develop, test, qualify, and help bring dependable building systems to consumers.

Our approach

We define the system being evaluated, measure its performance, and document the results and their limits.

Window performance

Why window performance matters.

Windows bring daylight and views into a building, but they also transfer heat. In cold climates, that can increase heating demand and make the space near a window uncomfortable.

Vacuum insulated glass greatly reduces heat transfer through the glazing. The performance of the complete window also depends on the frame, cladding, edges, seals, and installation.

What vacuum glass does

Vacuum insulated glass, or VIG, uses two panes separated by a narrow vacuum gap. The vacuum and a low-emissivity coating work together to reduce heat transfer.

This allows high insulation performance in a thin glass unit. Whether it can be used in an existing frame depends on the specific glass unit, frame, and installation details.

How vacuum glass works

Four parts work together.

  1. The vacuum.The vacuum gap greatly reduces heat transfer by gas conduction and convection between the panes.
  2. The coating.A low-emissivity coating, often called low-e, reduces radiant heat transfer across the gap.
  3. The support pillars.Tiny pillars keep the panes apart under atmospheric pressure.
  4. The edge seal.An airtight seal around the perimeter maintains the vacuum. The pillars and edge seal still provide paths for some heat transfer.
What this can mean for a building

An effective window system can reduce heating demand and keep the indoor glass surface warmer in winter. Warmer glass improves comfort near the window and can reduce the risk of indoor condensation or frost.

Sound insulation depends on the complete glass and frame assembly and should be assessed using the product’s acoustic rating.

Understanding the ratings

R-value describes resistance to heat flow: higher is better. U-factor describes heat transfer: lower is better.

A center-of-glass value describes only the middle of the glazing. A whole-window rating also accounts for the frame and edges. When comparing ratings, check that they describe the same part of the assembly and use the same units and test basis.

N50 Qualified

What N50 Qualified means.

N50 Qualified is the qualification mark owned by North 50 Building Systems. It identifies a complete assembly that has met our documented requirements for its intended use.

The qualification covers a specific configuration: the combination of components and installation details evaluated together.

Each qualification record identifies the configuration and intended use, along with the requirements, test methods, analysis, results, and limits.

We apply the same requirements to systems being evaluated for the same intended use, including our own technologies. Fees cover testing and documentation. Qualification is earned by meeting the requirements.

Engineering and development

Frames developed for vacuum insulated glass.

North 50 has already developed at least three frame profiles specifically for use with vacuum insulated glass, or VIG. We use pultruded and extruded profiles in a range of materials.

We also clad frames with high-performance organic materials, including super-high-density cork.

We evaluate the profiles and cladding as part of a complete assembly, together with the glass, gaskets, seals, and installation. Our development priorities include thermal performance and practical requirements for manufacturing, installation, and maintenance.

N50 qualification applies to the defined assembly and its intended use, with the components, test results, and limits documented.

EdgeRay · In development

EdgeRay is a North 50 technology in development that uses infrared energy to warm the edge of a window assembly and deliver that energy inside the frame. Its goal is to improve comfort near windows and help manage cold glass edges.

Development work examines how the infrared source, receiver, glass, and frame perform together under actual conditions.

Any N50 qualification would apply to a specific configuration and intended use. North 50’s own technologies are evaluated against the same requirements as other systems being considered for that use.

Why Fairbanks

Why we test in Fairbanks.

Interior Alaska provides long winters, deep cold, and changing outdoor conditions. That makes Fairbanks the most demanding place to study how installed building systems perform over time.

Field measurements add practical evidence to laboratory results. They help us understand how components behave together under actual operating conditions.

Insulation slows heat transfer in either direction, so it matters in both heating and cooling. Suitability for another climate also depends on factors such as solar exposure, moisture, and local building requirements.
